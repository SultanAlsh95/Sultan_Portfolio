DIAGNOSIS AND TREATMENT OF MODERATE ANGINA: DECISION TREE MODEL
===============================================================

A cost-effectiveness decision tree in Excel comparing 15 strategies for
diagnosing and treating men aged 55 with moderate typical angina, from
the perspective of a cardiology department in Sheffield.

Each strategy combines a diagnostic choice with a treatment choice:

  Diagnosis    no test, exercise stress test (EST), coronary angiography (ANG)
  Treatment    no treatment (NT), medical management (MM), bypass surgery (CABG)

Written as coursework for the Evaluation of Health Care module, MSc Health
Economics, University of York. The model was built as a group exercise and
the report is individual work.


HOW TO FOLLOW THE PROJECT
-------------------------

  Step 1   data/Modelling diagnostic and treatment strategies for angina 2025.docx
           The case study brief: the decision problem, probabilities,
           costs and health states the model is built from.

  Step 2   scripts/01_V5_Tree.xlsx
           The model. Main sheets:
             Rosser-Watts matrix     health state valuations
             Discounting             discounted QALYs and costs
             Probabilities           prior and test probabilities
             TREE sheets             the decision tree (46 end points)
             DSA sheets              sensitivity analyses: willingness to
                                     pay, discount rate, perfect care
             The "Tipping Point"     procedural death probabilities

  Step 3   scripts/02_AnginaReport_by_Sultan.pdf
           The written report: methods, results, sensitivity analysis
           and policy implications.


RESULT
------

At 35,000 pounds per QALY and a 3.5% discount rate, 12 of the 15
strategies are dominated. Angiography followed by bypass surgery for
positive results is the cost-effective strategy, with an ICER of 2,554
pounds per QALY compared with an exercise stress test followed by
medical management.

Methods, assumptions and limitations are in the report.
